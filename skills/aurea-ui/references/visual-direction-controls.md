# Visual Direction Controls

Use for a new website, major redesign, or a brief asking for stronger art direction or animation. These controls describe project intent; they are not scores of design quality. Infer them from the brief and existing brand, and explain the choice in one short sentence before implementation. The user's explicit preference wins.

## Three independent controls

| Control | Low | Medium | High |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Layout expression | Familiar hierarchy, aligned grid | Editorial contrast, selective asymmetry | Experimental composition and distinctive media placement |
| Motion intensity | Immediate feedback and subtle transitions | Visible hero/section choreography and microinteractions | A few cinematic scroll or spatial sequences |
| Content density | Spacious narrative | Balanced reading and scanning | Compact operational information |

Do not automatically set all three high. A dense dashboard can have precise low-motion feedback; a spacious portfolio can use cinematic motion. For an existing product, use its current settings as the starting point.

Example design read: “An editorial product launch: medium layout expression, medium motion with one hero demonstration, and low density; compact controls and static mobile storytelling.”

## Make expression concrete

Before choosing a hero, consider two plausible compositions based on the available content: product demonstration first, editorial split, full-bleed media with readable copy, typographic manifesto, or an interactive workspace preview. Choose by message, asset quality, reading order, and responsive behavior. Do not randomize layouts to manufacture originality.

Vary section composition by narrative role: explain with a sequence, compare with aligned rows, prove with real output, and invite action with a concise CTA. Avoid repeating the same text-left/image-right section merely because it is convenient. Repetition is appropriate for comparable items such as product plans or results.

Choose type for brand voice and actual language coverage. Test Vietnamese diacritics, headings, long labels, and numbers if Vietnamese content is expected. No universal font blacklist or mandatory dark theme: existing brand and user choice take precedence.

## Motion commitment

For medium/high motion, name the visible moments to implement and their triggers. A motion brief needs observable results: entry, scroll, hover/focus, state change, or a spatial demonstration. “Use GSAP” alone is not a motion specification.

Choose a small set of signature moments in line with interaction-motion.md. Routine feedback is separate from that budget. The design must remain expressive through typography, composition, and assets when reduced motion is enabled.

For each signature moment record: purpose, trigger, animated element/property, timing, interruption behavior, mobile behavior, reduced-motion alternative, and verification action. Numeric timing is a starting point to tune in the browser, not a universal rule.

If the effect cannot be completed within the available environment, deliver the usable fallback and identify the unfinished effect. Do not describe a static screenshot or imported animation package as evidence that motion works.
